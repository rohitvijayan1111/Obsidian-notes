Good approach — instead of trying to write the ratings cold, work backwards from _evidence_. For each of the 9 categories, here's the kind of concrete stuff from your recent months to dig up, so your self-assessment reads as fact-based rather than self-praised or vague.

**1. Technical Excellence**

- List the modules/tasks you owned end-to-end, roughly how much lead review each needed, and any bugs found post-delivery.
- Times you learned a new tool/language/framework mid-project.
- Any complex/ambiguous technical problem you solved without escalating.

**2. Big Picture Thinking**

- Instances where you asked a clarifying question _before_ starting that changed the approach or avoided rework.
- Any time you suggested an improvement to the plan/strategy, not just the execution.
- A case where you connected a small task back to the larger project goal unprompted.

**3. Consistent Delivery**

- Your actual estimate-vs-actual record for a few tasks (even rough memory helps).
- Any risks you flagged early, and what mitigation you proposed.
- Coding standard / process adherence — any specific feedback you got and whether it recurred or you fixed it.

**4. Responsiveness to Special Circumstances**

- A production issue, deadline crunch, or vague/changing requirement you had to navigate.
- Did you resolve it mostly independently, or did the lead have to step in? Be honest — this shapes which band fits.

**5. Core Values / TOWOD / Conflict Management**

- Any missed deadline and whether you flagged it in advance or it surfaced late.
- A disagreement or piece of critical feedback you received — how you responded (defensively vs. openly, did you ask clarifying questions, did you act on it afterward).
- Ownership examples: something that went wrong that you owned rather than deflected.

**6. Leadership & Initiatives**

- Times you helped a peer without being asked.
- Any knowledge-sharing (internal talk, doc, code walkthrough) you did.
- Whether you were bringing things to the lead for direction, or resolving things at your level.

**7. Enhancing Team Health**

- Participation in team syncs/socials — active or passive?
- Any time you helped smooth over a minor team friction or boosted morale.

**8. Communication Skill**

- Feedback you've gotten (formal or offhand) about your written/verbal clarity.
- Instances where you proactively raised a concern rather than waiting to be asked.
- Any case where you helped bridge a communication gap between people.

**9. Innovation**

- Any suggestion you made that went beyond the literal ask — and whether it got implemented.
- A recurring pain point you noticed and proposed fixing (even if small).

A practical way to gather this: skim your last 3–6 months of **sprint boards/Jira tickets, PR/code review comments, 1:1 notes, retro notes, and Slack/email threads** where you got praise or corrective feedback. Specific, dated examples ("in the March X integration, I flagged the API rate-limit risk two days before it became a blocker") will make your self-rating far more defensible than general impressions.
